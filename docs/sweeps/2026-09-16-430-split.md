## Scoring note: these rows carry no scores

Promotion via `add-product` is where openness, adoption and capability are researched, and where
the license body of each product is read rather than its repository label. The discovery pass
flagged two products whose label misleads in opposite directions:

- **`document_conversion`: `chandra`, `surya`.** The repository label reads Apache-2.0, and the
  code is; the Datalab model weights carry a modified OpenRAIL-M license, free below USD 2M
  funding or revenue and forbidden competitively against Datalab's own API. That is the
  `competition_restricted` tier stated directly, and it is why `marker`, the same vendor's
  product already on the map, scores where it does.
- **`document_conversion`: `pymupdf4llm`.** AGPL-3.0-or-later in the body, with a commercial
  Artifex license as the alternative — stricter than a reader skimming for a permissive label
  expects.
- **`search_retrieval`: `serpapi`, `linkup`.** Both publish MIT-licensed client SDKs in front of
  a closed index. The SDK is not the product, and scoring either as MIT would repeat the
  measurement-population error `langfuse` was corrected for. The rows declare the package
  because it is the addressable artifact; promotion must score the service.

**PyMuPDF is not listed in `document_conversion`.** `pymupdf/PyMuPDF` is already a tail candidate
in `sources/registry/dataset_processing_tools.yaml` as `pymupdf`, and one artifact belongs to one
row — `validate` rejected the duplicate, which is the check working. The AGPL-3.0-or-later body
noted above for `pymupdf4llm` still applies to `pymupdf` wherever it is eventually promoted, and
whether a text-layer extractor belongs in `document_conversion` or in corpus processing is a
promotion-time boundary call rather than a reason to register it twice.
